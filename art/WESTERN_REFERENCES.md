# Western location art: references and interpretation

`western_locations.gd` authors 30 new route-stop images, a separate pre-construction Kearny image, and three travel landscapes on the same native grid and palette as the opening. Every route arrival has a registry entry; art coverage tests reject missing or duplicate images. Foreground wagons, people, viewpoint, colors and most placement are authored interpretation. No proprietary game art or scanned image is pasted or traced.

## Written references and explicit limits

- Fort Kearny: [NPS site history](https://www.nps.gov/places/000/fort-kearny-state-historical-park.htm) supports June 1848 construction and an open sod/adobe camp. The page's modern stockade photo was **not** used as evidence of an 1848 wall. Before June, the alternate illustration omits the sod/adobe work.
- Fort John: [NPS Fort Laramie FAQ](https://www.nps.gov/fola/faqs.htm?fullweb=1) distinguishes the 1841 adobe post from the later army complex. No Old Bedlam or parade-ground army buildings are drawn.
- Fort Bridger: [NPS site history](https://home.nps.gov/mopi/planyourvisit/fort-bridger.htm) and the Wyoming SHPO report describe an early trading establishment. Our small enclosure/cabins are interpretive; the 1858 army complex is absent.
- Fort Hall: [NPS historic-site survey](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/4fee6836-0a61-47b2-a9f5-c6779a469177) describes rebuilding in adobe around 1840. The image is a schematic enclosure, not a verified architectural elevation or the modern replica.
- Fort Boise: [NPS old-post account](https://www.nps.gov/places/fort-boise-site.htm) describes the adobe trading post near the Boise/Snake confluence. Its 1851 building list is later comparative evidence, not silently treated as an 1848 plan. No 1863 military Boise buildings are drawn.
- Scotts Bluff: [NPS Robidoux Pass](https://www.nps.gov/places/robidoux-pass.htm) informs the route around the obstruction. No later Mitchell Pass wagon cut is depicted.
- South Pass: [NPS landscape description](https://www.nps.gov/cali/planyourvisit/south-pass.htm) supports the wide, low saddle. The crossing image deliberately omits an implausible narrow high-alpine notch.
- Courthouse/Jail Rocks: [History Nebraska](https://history.nebraska.gov/courthouse-and-jail-rocks/) and Palmer's table/editorial identification support the paired landmarks and Solitary Tower name. Shapes and camera distance remain simplified.
- Ash Hollow, Chimney Rock, Independence Rock, Devil's Gate, the river corridors, Powder/Grande Ronde valleys and Blue Mountains use Palmer's descriptions and geographic context. Their silhouettes/vegetation are interpretations rather than independently verified 1848 panoramas. A lone pine is not revived at Palmer's already-named stump.
- Barlow Pass, Laurel Hill and Eagle Creek use [Oregon Historical Society's road description](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/barlow_road/). The hill/rope scene conveys the documented type of descent, not a measured profile or an assertion that one precise tree was used. Mount Hood and the forests provide location cues.

No image alone proves service availability, travel time, tolls or hazard rates. Those belong to the route/simulation evidence ledger. Reference years, depiction years, guide dates and our game setting are kept distinct.

## Local Native community encounter drafts (October27,2026)

See [the encounter art brief](../docs/NATIVE_ENCOUNTER_ART_BRIEF.md) for seven separate1848 scenes and evidence limits. `aseprite/scenes/encounter_wasco.lua` is an original landing composition approved by Spencer on October27,2026 and installed as `pixels/encounter_the_dalles.png`. The remaining six encounters are briefed only. Primary community context: [Warm Springs history](https://warmsprings-nsn.gov/history/), [Pawnee homeland](https://pawneenation.org/historical-marker-pawnee-marker-nebraska/), [Shoshone-Bannock history](https://www.sbtribes.com/about/), [CTUIR history](https://ctuir.org/about/brief-history-of-ctuir/). [NPS Columbia report](https://www.nps.gov/oreg/learn/historyculture/upload/HRA_Columbia_River_Narrative_History_Final_200917-reduced-size.pdf) supplies crossing/portage context.

For detail review, [Warm Springs community arts and basketry](https://www.lib.uidaho.edu/digital/L3/ShowOneObjectSiteID66ObjectID981.html) distinguishes nineteenth-century objects from later demonstrations and describes everyday storage/fishing materials. [ROM's Kane publication account](https://www.rom.on.ca/rom-publications/wanderings-artist-among-indians-north-america) establishes late1840s travel but not accuracy of every later painting. The current plain garments, baskets and canoe silhouette are provisional; no patterns or sacred designs are copied. No later reservation architecture, generic warbonnets, locks or railway portage infrastructure.
