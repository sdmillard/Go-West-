# Hunting activity

**Current update:** [Economy and preservation](ECONOMY_AND_PRESERVATION.md) adds usable post-hunt drying, optional salt, finite preserved-food expiry, separate dried cargo weight and 3,600 matched full-road policy journeys. The 100 lb hunting return cap is unchanged.

**Current food/load update:** see [Food and survival baseline](FOOD_AND_SURVIVAL.md) for the 800 lb suggested outfit, total cargo, variable hunting yields and fresh-meat spoilage. Earlier balance numbers below are archived observations.

Open **Options → Hunt** between landmarks with ammunition available. Left-click to fire; right-click to walk to a point. Walk around cover to open a clear shooting lane. Walking stops at cover rather than automatically finding a path around it. Each outing spends one trail day and that day's rations immediately; the 30-second activity does not spend additional trail days.

The independent `simulation/hunting.gd` model owns movement, cover collision, encounter generation and the activity clock. `simulation/trail_engine.gd` owns ammunition and food settlement. `ui/hunt_view.gd` draws original pixel animals, terrain cover and the hunter, and forwards mouse input. Animation and rendering use no random draws. Reloading lasts 1.4 seconds; rejected rapid clicks cost no ammunition. A valid shot spends one bullet whether it hits an animal, cover or empty ground. Gunfire makes surviving animals run faster; they leave the screen rather than wrapping around. Five shots, 30 seconds, exhausted ammunition or no remaining game end the activity; Return to Camp settles it once.

An outing generates zero to three animals, with repeated species possible. The opening corridor uses wooded-prairie and open-prairie habitat pools, with a heuristic change at the Kansas River's 81 guide miles. This is an authored simplification, not a reconstructed historical species boundary. Neither habitat guarantees a bison or any other game. Empty outings still cost a day. Four randomized cover objects block rifle fire and hunter movement. Their arrangement and the animals are reproducible from journey seed and outing number, with a separate generator that does not perturb travel/event randomness.

`data/hunting.json` defines the duration, reload, shot/carry limits, animal dimensions/speeds, usable-meat yields and habitat weights. Invalid definitions fail on loading. Current per-animal usable-meat ranges are bison 200–450 lb, deer 42–85 lb and cottontail 1–2 lb. They represent provisional recoverable food, **not live animal weight or measured historical dressing yields**. Only 100 lb, or remaining cargo space if smaller, can return; the journal records shots, obtained/carried meat, habitat and meat left behind. Fresh meat is now tracked by age, eaten before staples and subject to spoilage. Drying and carcass retrieval are not modeled. The return dialog reports actual meat obtained, carried and left behind.

## Evidence and adaptations

[R. Philip Bouchard's designer account](https://www.philipbouchard.com/oregon-trail/hunting.html) describes third-person hunting, terrain obstacles, geographical animal pools, varied terrain objects and the deliberately chosen 100-pound carrying cap. Those principles inform this version; mouse movement, instantaneous aimed shots, reload duration and encounter odds are our adaptations. No original game art is reused. The full classic five-zone hunting system is not claimed: only the built opening corridor is represented.

[Johnson and Winter's original 1846 advice, in a later reprint](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Route_across_the_Rocky_Mountains_with_a_Description_of_Oregon_and_California_(1982)/Appendix) warns emigrants to bring provisions and not depend on buffalo. It provides no statistical encounter model. Our empty-hunt probability and relative animal weights are game tuning, not historical measurements. BASIC's timed typing, 40-bullet prerequisite and food-expenditure rewards remain documented adaptations rather than pound-for-pound reference values.

## Verification and balance

`tests/test_hunting.gd` exercises reproducible encounters, habitat choice, empty outings, cover shots, reload/ammunition accounting, movement collision, animal escape, time expiry and exactly-once settlement. The graphical survival suite clicks a real target through the actual mouse input path. Existing complete-opening-chapter policy regressions also exercise the revised encounters.

A 600-run experiment (same seeds, months and budgets as balance pass 01) is archived in `hunting-balance-results.json`. The scripted hunter observes current animals, advances the reload clock, and has a 65% shot success policy; it does not solve cover or reproduce a human aiming session. Cautious parties reached the chapter end with all five members in 120/120 runs, averaging 37.1 days and 3.0 hunts versus 35.2 days and 1.0 hunt in the earlier fixed-target experiment. Bare-ration parties retained all five in 80/120 runs, versus 93/120 previously. These are regression/tuning observations, not historical mortality estimates or a final human difficulty assessment. Older balance reports remain archived snapshots.
