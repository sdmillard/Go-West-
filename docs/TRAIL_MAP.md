# Trail map

The MAP button (top right) opens a map of the West in 1848. Every road in `data/route.json` is drawn dotted, the roads the party has taken are drawn in red, and a small wagon with a YOU ARE HERE tag marks where the party stands, partway along the current road when it is between stops. Hovering a stop names it. Opening the map pauses travel.

## Geography

- The picture is `art/pixels/trail_map.png` (616 x 288), drawn by `art/aseprite/scenes/trail_map.lua`. It shows the coast, natural lakes, rivers, hill shading lit from the upper left, 5° graticule, the Cascade volcanoes and Laramie Peak, and the 1848 boundaries: the 49th parallel (1846), the 42nd parallel (Mexican Cession, February 1848), Missouri (with the Platte Purchase) and Iowa (1846). Reservoirs are left out; only natural lakes are drawn.
- `art/aseprite/data/map_geo.lua` is generated by `art/aseprite/tools/make_map_geo.py` from Natural Earth vectors (public domain) and the AWS Terrain Tiles elevation data (open data; SRTM, GMTED, ETOPO1, USGS NED). The script's docstring lists the files and URLs. It needs Python with Pillow, NumPy and SciPy.
- `data/map.json` holds the projection (equirectangular, stretched for 43.5° N), each stop's latitude and longitude, waypoints that bend roads along the rivers they followed, peaks, boundaries and labels. The Lua scene and the generator read the same projection; change all three together.
- Stop positions come from present-day markers and NPS/BLM trail maps. Crossings sit at the crossing the guide used (Papin's ferry at Topeka, the lower South Platte crossing near Brule, the Mormon ferry near Casper, Three Island Crossing, old Fort Boise near Parma). Fort Kearny is the 1848 post on the Platte; Fort Walla Walla is the HBC fort at Wallula.

## Code

- `ui/map_view.gd` draws the roads, the journey and the wagon over the picture; the journey comes from `game.travelled_path()` plus the part of the current road given by `leg_miles / leg_length`.
- `tests/test_ui.gd` (`check_map`) checks that every stop is on the map, waypoints follow real roads, the branch taken is the one drawn, and the wagon stands partway along the current road.
