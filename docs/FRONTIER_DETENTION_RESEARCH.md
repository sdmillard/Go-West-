# Detention, trials and imprisonment on the 1848 route

Research checkpoint: October 27, 2026. Research and proposed design only; no detention mechanics, sentencing rules, sprites or saves were changed. Scope: our Independence–Oregon City route, with Missouri's penitentiary represented off-map if needed.

The central distinction is between arrest/temporary restraint, custody pending a hearing or transfer, and confinement imposed after conviction. The ability to restrain someone does not establish a functioning jail, a competent court or authority to impose a particular sentence. Conversely, a court can exist while lacking somewhere to carry out its imprisonment orders. A county jail can also serve sentences where law and facilities support that use; it is not inherently limited to pretrial detention.

## Findings that should govern implementation

### Missouri: a real county jail and an off-map penitentiary

**Supported:** Independence had a brick county jail built in 1841, before the surviving 1859 jail. The Jackson County Historical Society, which operates the later museum, gives this chronology. The 1859 building is not an appropriate 1848 scene reference. Missouri State Archives confirms that the penitentiary in Jefferson City opened in 1836 and preserves court commitment orders beginning that year. This establishes an available institution, not a tariff or mandatory sentence for every offense.

**Design implication:** a locally arrested accused traveler can be held by the county while a case is reviewed. Following a qualifying Missouri conviction, penitentiary transfer can be an off-map, journey-ending outcome for that person. It is not voluntary travel east, death, or serving years in Independence. A companion's transfer need not end the surviving party's journey.

**Unresolved:** exact 1848 Jackson County court dates, ordinary-offense sentence ranges, bail amounts and the 1841 jail's appearance. No flat bail/fine schedule or detailed jail facade is established by this pass. The existing five-guide-mile Missouri posse gate is an authored conservative game boundary, not the historical county/state boundary.

### Military custody: transfer is different from a fort sentencing court

**Primary law:** section 23 of the June 30,1834 Indian Trade and Intercourse Act (4 Stat.729,733; digital PDF p4) authorizes specified military apprehensions under that act and removal to civil authority. Its proviso limits detention to five days after arrest **before removal**. It does not require trial within five days, make every captor an officer, authorize indefinite confinement at a fort, or make expiration an automatic acquittal. The provision has a particular statutory scope; it is not a universal 1848 arrest rule.

Sections24–25 address judicial attachment and criminal jurisdiction, including an exception concerning offenses between Indigenous people. Their 1834 geography and later legal developments need venue-specific treatment; do not blindly apply the original map description to all Oregon in 1848. The March 3,1847 amendment (9 Stat.203, section 2) supplies a useful contemporary example: specified liquor offenses could lead to imprisonment following conviction in the proper U.S. district court. A statutory prison penalty did not mean the nearest trading fort was a prison. This example is evidence for the legal distinction, not a proposal to add liquor trade to our current offense catalog.

**Design implication:** gated military custody can lead to civil transfer. Where section 23 actually applies, its pre-removal limit is a documented starting point. Other holding windows require their own basis or must be labeled authored scheduling. Do not send every federally arrested person automatically to Missouri's state penitentiary; jurisdiction, court and final place of imprisonment are separate decisions.

### The middle route: most forts were trading establishments

**Supported:** Fort Laramie was privately operated in 1848; the Army purchase came in 1849. Fort Bridger was a trading post in 1848, with its military phase later in the1850s. Fort Hall and the old Fort Boise were Hudson's Bay Company trading establishments. Cantonment Loring near Fort Hall belongs to 1849 and is a distinct later post; the later military Fort Boise is also not the old trading establishment.

**Design implication:** armed staff can defend a post; a particular authored incident may involve brief guarded restraint, negotiation or an attempt to find an appropriate authority. Company ownership alone neither proves nor disproves an individual's legal commission. No standing 1848 criminal court or penitentiary has been verified at these four stops in this research. Default them to **unverified formal custody**, not universal army guardhouses or an assertion that nobody could ever be restrained there. Named magistrates or commissions need individual evidence.

### Fort Kearny: date-sensitive military presence, uncertain holding structure

**Supported:** the Platte post began construction inJune 1848; many of that year's emigrants had passed earlier. NPS describes the following year's establishment as a sparse camp of tents and a few earthen buildings. Its early military presence supports a military-post profile once active, but does not authenticate an 1848 guardhouse or establish that an officer tried every civilian offense.

**Design implication:** preserve the game's existing pre-June restriction. A guarded camp/room is a plausible authored presentation only when a custodian is actually present; do not copy later fort buildings. Specific military arrest/transfer authority still needs to match the alleged offense. Do not treat military court-martial powers over soldiers as general civilian sentencing powers.

### Oregon City: civil government and courts, but insecure capacity

**Primary evidence:** Governor Abernethy's December 7,1847 message (State Archives transcription of the 1853 printed Oregon Archives; digital p11) says the judiciary was working while imprisonment orders could not be carried out for lack of a prison. The State Archives penitentiary guide separately records an 1845 Oregon City jail, its 1846 destruction, later rudimentary county confinement and a territorial penitentiary becoming usable in 1856.

The iron-box confinement described in the archival guide has no precise start date there. It must not be treated as a verified 1848 building merely because it existed before the mid1850s. More general corrections histories use different founding dates; those summaries do not settle which structure operated in our year. The contemporaneous 1847 complaint is the stronger warning against depicting a developed 1848 prison.

**Design implication:** Oregon City can have a provisional civil-authority/court profile, with uncertain or makeshift holding represented cautiously. Do not create a working Portland/Salem penitentiary for 1848, or silently convert an unexecutable imprisonment order into automatic flogging. Courts, enforcement and capacity must be independent capabilities. Exact 1848 arrangements remain an open research item.

The federal Organic Act created Oregon Territory inAugust 1848, but the new governor assumed duties onMarch 3,1849; the provisional administration continued in the interval. An August date alone should not spawn a complete territorial enforcement apparatus.

### Oregon militia and emigrant tribunals: distinct actors

The western route was not devoid of organized coercive force. Abernethy's 1849 message describes the 1848 militia campaign, withdrawal inJuly and a small force at Waiilatpu until midSeptember. A federal BPA cultural-resources report identifies Fort Lee at The Dalles in early 1848. These are not evidence of a permanent criminal jail/court at each site. Do not merge HBC Fort Walla Walla with the militia position at Waiilatpu. Month-level presence and exact locations need verification before enabling routine militia arrest events.

The Oregon Historical Society's Oregon Trail synthesis describes wagon-company rules and tribunals, expulsion and guard duties, with more severe punishments also reported. It spans multiple trail years. It supports a distinct emigrant-tribunal abstraction, not an exact 1848 sentencing table or a modern lawful court at every camp. Private reward seekers likewise do not become sheriffs just because a reward is offered.

Indigenous nations had their own laws and authorities. Neither a blanket claim of lawlessness nor a generic Indigenous jail/court profile is justified. Community-specific detention or adjudication requires further community-specific evidence.

## Audit of the current game

- `simulation/wanted_system.gd`, `trial()`, currently maps the worst known severity directly to a common sentence across captors: restitution/robbery/assault/hanging. A known killing leads directly to the leader being hanged. That is authored gameplay, not a demonstrated location-specific legal process.
- The same function can flog a captured leader for severity2–3 and banish helpers. Voluntary surrender avoids flogging. Neither a uniform sentence nor that automatic distinction has been established as 1848 law.
- `simulation/crime_system.gd`, `result()`, routes captures/surrenders into that common trial function. Merely inserting a jail timer before it would preserve the historical problem.
- `simulation/outing_encounters.gd` handles bandits. `docs/FIELD_OUTINGS.md` explicitly records that companion arrest, custody and trials have not been implemented. There is no existing held-companion subsystem to extend in this checkout.
- News, arrived reports, participation and identified witnesses are useful foundations. A Criminal background alone is not evidence; a party accusation also must not automatically convict every companion or execute the current leader for someone else's act.

The first correction should therefore be an authority/venue and accused-person stage before sentencing, followed by custody state. A hearing must permit insufficient evidence, release or referral where supported. “A killing is known” is not a complete determination of murder, individual responsibility or a legally justified execution. This is a correction to attribution and process, not a guarantee of leniency.

## Proposed implementation order

1. **Authority and venue routing.** Add data profiles with active dates, captor type, region, supported legal basis, holding capacity/confidence, hearing venue and permitted dispositions. Start with the Missouri case and a narrow active-Kearny transfer case. Keep unknown trading-post courts disabled. Separate state/civil review, military transfer, informal tribunal and private extortion/settlement.
2. **One individual's temporary custody.** Record actual accused ids, specific incident/evidence ids, custodian, physical holding position, arrest day, review/transfer deadline and separate party knowledge day. Treat the held person as unavailable for hauling, hunting, camp tasks, nursing and driving. They remain alive with history; temporary custody reserves their return position. Promote an available leader if necessary. The party does not instantly learn an unseen outrider's fate.
3. **A concrete intervention encounter.** On arrival, show the accusation, custodian and time remaining. Allow evidence/testimony, a supported appearance-security/bail process, or a lawful settlement/fine where applicable. Bail/security is not conviction, restitution or a universal fee that buys innocence. A private payoff does not settle all public charges. Escape/fighting can initially use existing dangerous event outcomes; tactical prison breaks remain a later combat feature.
4. **Referral, trial and sentence.** Separate hearing from verdict, and verdict from execution of a sentence. Long off-map confinement removes that traveler from the active journey while preserving an alive/imprisoned history; it is not a death or a two-day wait that completes a years-long sentence. Existing resolved and terminal legacy saves stay resolved. Exact statutory sentence and bail tables remain blocked on additional source work, rather than filled with invented historical numbers.
5. **Follow-up coverage.** Add informal regulator custody and carefully dated Oregon civil/militia cases once the simple path works. New confinement artwork needs the existing Aseprite review process and year-correct references.

For an authored encounter, a short review window can be chosen for playability, but must be described as that event's schedule rather than a historic universal duration. The documented five-day pre-removal rule is available only for cases genuinely falling under the 1834 provision. It is not a trial clock or a universal promise of release. Transfers need actual destinations and elapsed travel, not teleportation between posts. A custodian's provisioning and confiscated goods need explicit accounting; prisoners cannot draw food remotely from the wagon for free or consume it twice.

## Implementation acceptance checks

- No formal arrest/hearing profile before its active date, at the wrong location, or without an applicable authority.
- Distinguish custody, bail/security, verdict, sentence, transfer and release in state and UI.
- Match charges to individual participation and known evidence; no trait-only guilt or universal automatic hanging/flogging.
- Held people cannot contribute tasks/capacity/teams, die and later return, or be recruited twice. Successor leadership and all-party custody need explicit handling.
- Different food/cash/goods ledgers for the party, seized property, appearance security and any custodian board; no double loss or invented stock.
- Persistent map position and transfer destination; intervention costs real journey days, and information follows existing news delays.
- Save/load preserves custody deadlines, released charges and irreversible transfers. Old instant-trial/terminal outcomes are not reopened.
- One event cannot issue the same sentence or collect the same fine twice. A settlement with one private party does not erase unrelated accusations.
