# Forage evidence and edible food sharing

October 26, 2026. Follow-up to the [624-journey comparison](FEEDING_SURVIVAL_COMPARISON.md).

**New journeys now keep edible partial servings for people unless the player explicitly enables feeding them to hens.** Scratching and poultry grain feeding continue normally. The Livestock checkbox says “Feed edible food portions to hens” and explains that these are human supplies, used before grain. Existing saves retain their explicit on/off choices. Older records without the setting retain their previous effective behavior (on). No existing party receives food, feed, healing or money through this change.

This is a resource-allocation default, not a claim that hens cannot eat scraps. The prior default could feed the edible food saved by eggs back to hens. In the separate exploratory comparison, changing the setting alone did not produce arrivals. It therefore does not establish that farmer balance is solved.

## What the feeding references establish

Sources checked October 26, 2026. Modern intake and nutrient references are physiological analogues; historical interpretation describes trail conditions. Neither supplies a measured 1848 daily ration for our particular animals.

## Why the regional percentages are not changed in this patch

The current 25 lb cow / 3 lb goat values are called nominal dry-ration allowances in the rules, while carried fodder is bought and hauled as physical pounds. The model treats these as equivalent, without feed moisture or digestibility. Twenty-five pounds of dry matter differs from twenty-five pounds of hay. For illustration only, at an assumed 90% dry matter, 25 lb hay would contain 22.5 lb dry matter; that assumption is not a measured property of the game's fodder.

This means the reference tables do **not** justify simply lowering the cow ration from 25 to 20, or making 75% modeled forage support full milk. Nor do they prove the existing ration is too small or large for every trail cow. A steep drop near maintenance is biologically plausible; the particular common 70%/95% thresholds, animal sizes, food quality and habitat caps are not independently calibrated. The previous result identifies a sensitivity, not proof that all the drop is erroneous.

The controlled high-country goat reaching zero milk is mechanically explained by its 70% intake cap equalling maintenance. There is no cited measurement tying the whole Sweetwater/South Pass corridor to precisely that cap. July's single global season cap also cannot represent green higher-elevation forage and dried lowland forage simultaneously. Raising every cap until farmers win would hide these modeling omissions.

A useful next model should specify a representative body size for each species, feed moisture/digestible-energy basis, maintenance plus milk demand (including the taper already simulated), and a modest travel-energy allowance. Local forage quantity/quality should then be assigned by supported route zones and season, with explicit poor/waterless stretches and supplements. Seasonal quantity, animal condition and lactation must not charge the same shortfall twice. This can stay internal; players need readable feed coverage and estimated milk, not nutrient arithmetic.

Before adopting that broader change, use controlled sensitivity fixtures for plausible body sizes and feed quality, comparing Fair/Good, rest/travel, early/late lactation, good/depleted forage and supplementation. Keep maxima at the existing 8 lb cow / 2 lb goat / 0.5 egg allowances unless separate yield evidence changes. Then freeze the candidate rules and compare fresh journeys, keeping ration/dispatch/spending policy experiments separate. Existing biological rates, ration masses, prices, regional caps and starting funds remain unchanged here.
